\begin{helper}
Let \(q,t,A\) be natural numbers.  Suppose the number of children marked by
the popular-\(b\) rule is at most \(128tq^t\), and the number marked by the
poor-\(a\) rule is at most \(10000tq^t\).  If \(A\ge10128t\), then the total
number of marked children is at most
\[
  Aq^t.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Adding the two marking contributions gives
\[
  \#\mathrm{popular}+\#\mathrm{poor}
  \le128tq^t+10000tq^t
  =(128+10000)tq^t
  =10128tq^t.
\]
Since \(A\ge10128t\), multiplying by the nonnegative integer \(q^t\) gives
\[
  10128tq^t\le Aq^t.
\]
The desired bound follows by transitivity.
\end{proof}
