\begin{helper}
Let \(Z\) be a nonempty finite set, let \(W(y)\) be a finite set of points
for each \(y\in Z\), and let \(P\) be a finite set of popular points.  Assume
\(q>0\).  Suppose every \(b\in P\) lies in at least
\[
  |Z|/(16q)
\]
of the sets \(W(y)\), and suppose that for every \(y\in Z\) the set
\(W(y)\) contains at most
\[
  2q^\ell/q
\]
points of \(P\).  Then
\[
  |P|\le 32q^\ell .
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Double-count the incidence set
\[
  I=\{(b,y): b\in P,\ y\in Z,\ b\in W(y)\}.
\]
Counting first by \(b\), the popularity assumption gives
\[
  |I|=\sum_{b\in P}|\{y\in Z:b\in W(y)\}|
      \ge |P|\cdot |Z|/(16q).
\]
Counting first by \(y\), the fiber-size assumption gives
\[
  |I|=\sum_{y\in Z}|W(y)\cap P|
      \le |Z|\cdot 2q^\ell/q.
\]
Since \(|Z|>0\) and \(q>0\), we may divide the resulting inequality
\[
  |P|\cdot |Z|/(16q)\le |Z|\cdot 2q^\ell/q
\]
by the positive factor \(|Z|/q\).  This yields
\[
  |P|\le 16\cdot 2q^\ell=32q^\ell .
\]
\end{proof}
