\begin{helper}
Let \(K\) be a finite field of order \(q\), let \(p\) be a prefix, and fix a
rank \(r\le t\).  The number of legal popular children \(x=(a,b)\) of \(p\),
in the sense of \(\noderef{StarProductPopularChild}\),
whose second coordinate has prefix rank \(r^p(b)=r\) is at most
\[
  64q^t .
\]
Popularity is with respect to the layer choice
\(\ell^p_r=\noderef{StarProductLayerChoice}\).
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Let \(B=\operatorname{Pop}^p_r\), the set of popular second coordinates from
\(\noderef{StarProductPopularSecondCoordinates}\).  By
\(\noderef{StarProductPopularSecondCoordinatesBound}\),
\[
  |B|\le 32q^{\ell^p_r}.
\]
For a fixed \(b\in B\), the defining membership \(b\in Z^p_r\) from
\(\noderef{StarProductRankLayer}\) gives \(r^p(b)=r\).  Since \(r\le t\),
the fixed-second-coordinate estimate
\(\noderef{StarProductFixedBRankLeExtensionBound}\), together with the
geometric-series estimate \(\noderef{StarProductGeometricSumLeTwoPow}\),
shows that there are at most \(2q^{t-r}\) legal choices of the first
coordinate \(a\) extending \(p\) with this fixed \(b\).  A legal child with
fixed \(b\) is determined by its first coordinate, so there are at most
\(2q^{t-r}\) such children.

Thus the rank-\(r\) popular children are covered by the union, over
\(b\in B\), of sets of size at most \(2q^{t-r}\).  Hence their number is at
most
\[
  |B|\cdot 2q^{t-r}
  \le 32q^{\ell^p_r}\cdot 2q^{t-r}.
\]
By \(\noderef{StarProductLayerChoiceLe}\), \(\ell^p_r\le r\).  Since
\(q>0\), \(q^{\ell^p_r}\le q^r\), and therefore
\[
  32q^{\ell^p_r}\cdot 2q^{t-r}
  \le 64q^r q^{t-r}
  =64q^t.
\]
\end{proof}
