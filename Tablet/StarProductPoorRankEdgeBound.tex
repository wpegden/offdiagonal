\begin{helper}
Let \(K\) be a finite field of order \(q\), let \(t\ge2\), and fix a
prefix \(p\).  For \(r\le t\), put
\[
  Z_\ell=Z^p_{\ell^p_r},
  \qquad \ell^p_r=\noderef{StarProductLayerChoice},
\]
and let
\[
  P_r=\{a:\ |N_{G(t,K)}(a)\cap Z_\ell|\le |Z_\ell|/(8q)\}.
\]
Then, with \(Z_r=Z^p_r\) as in \(\noderef{StarProductRankLayer}\),
\[
  e_{G(t,K)}(P_r,Z_r)\le 5000q^t .
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Let \(G=G(t,K)\).  By the definition of \(P_r\), for every
\(a\in P_r\),
\[
  |N_G(a)\cap Z_\ell|\le |Z_\ell|/(8q).
\]
Summing this inequality over all \(a\in P_r\), and using the edge-count
definition \(\noderef{LoopGraphEdgeCountBetween}\), gives
\[
  e_G(P_r,Z_\ell)\le |P_r||Z_\ell|/(8q). \tag{1}
\]

Set
\[
  X=|P_r||Z_\ell|,\qquad Y=|P_r||Z_r|.
\]
The layer choice is maximal among \(Z^p_s\) for \(s\le r\).  Applying
\(\noderef{StarProductLayerChoiceMax}\) with \(s=r\) gives
\[
  |Z_r|\le |Z_\ell|,
\]
and hence \(Y\le X\).

Let \(n,d,\lambda\) be the polarity parameters from
\(\noderef{PolarityGraphParameters}\).  The arithmetic bounds of
\(\noderef{StarProductPolarityParameterBounds}\) give
\[
  d/n\ge 1/(4q),\qquad d/n\le 4/q,\qquad \lambda^2=q^{t-1}.
\]
Applying \(\noderef{ExpanderMixingLemma}\) to \((P_r,Z_\ell)\) and using
\(d/n\ge1/(4q)\) yields
\[
  X/(4q)-\lambda\sqrt X\le e_G(P_r,Z_\ell). \tag{2}
\]
Applying the same lemma to \((P_r,Z_r)\) and using \(d/n\le4/q\) yields
\[
  e_G(P_r,Z_r)\le (4/q)Y+\lambda\sqrt Y. \tag{3}
\]

From \(\lambda^2=q^{t-1}\) and \(q>0\), we have
\[
  64q^2\lambda^2\le1024q^{t+1}
\]
and
\[
  \lambda\sqrt{1024q^{t+1}}\le904q^t.
\]
Now \((1)\), \((2)\), \(Y\le X\), \((3)\), and the two displayed numerical
estimates are exactly the hypotheses of
\(\noderef{StarProductPoorExpanderMixingBound}\).  That helper gives
\[
  e_G(P_r,Z_r)\le5000q^t,
\]
as required.
\end{proof}
