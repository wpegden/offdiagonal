\begin{helper}
Let \(a>0\) and \(B>0\).  Then, for all sufficiently large integers \(k\),
\[
  B\le a\,\frac{k}{(\log k)^2}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
The standard estimate \((\log x)^2=o(x)\) gives, with
\(\epsilon=a/B>0\), a threshold after which
\[
  (\log x)^2\le \frac aB x.
\]
Increase the threshold so that also \(\log x\ge1\).  Then for every integer
\(k\) beyond the threshold, \((\log k)^2>0\), and multiplying the displayed
inequality by \(B\) gives
\[
  B(\log k)^2\le ak.
\]
Division by the positive number \((\log k)^2\) proves the claim.
\end{proof}
