\begin{helper}
Let \(N,\rho\in\mathbb R\) and let \(e\) be a nonnegative integer.  Suppose
\[
  N\ge0,\qquad 0\le\rho\le1,\qquad N\rho^e<1.
\]
Then for every integer \(c\ge e\),
\[
  N\rho^c<1.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Because \(0\le\rho\le1\), powers of \(\rho\) are nonincreasing in the
exponent.  Thus \(c\ge e\) implies
\[
  \rho^c\le\rho^e.
\]
Multiplying by the nonnegative number \(N\) gives
\[
  N\rho^c\le N\rho^e<1,
\]
as required.
\end{proof}
