\begin{helper}
Let \(K\) be a finite field of order \(q\), let \(t\ge2\), fix a prefix
\(p\), and let \(r\le t\).  Among legal forward-independent children
\(x=(a,b)\) of \(p\) whose second coordinate has rank \(r\), at most
\[
  5000q^t
\]
are poor in the sense of \(\noderef{StarProductPoorChild}\) for the
concrete layer choice \(\ell^p_r=\noderef{StarProductLayerChoice}\).
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Let \(Z_\ell=Z^p_{\ell^p_r}\), and let
\[
  P_r=\{a:\ |N_{G(t,K)}(a)\cap Z_\ell|\le |Z_\ell|/(8q)\}.
\]
This is exactly the first-coordinate poor set appearing in
\(\noderef{StarProductPoorRankEdgeBound}\).

Take a legal poor child \(x=(a,b)\) of rank \(r\).  Since \(x\) is a vertex
of the product digraph, \(\noderef{ProductDigraphVertex}\) says
\(ab\in E(G(t,K))\).  Since \(x\) is poor and its second-coordinate rank is
\(r\), the definition \(\noderef{StarProductPoorChild}\) says precisely that
\[
  a\in P_r.
\]
The rank condition says \(b\in Z_r\), by
\(\noderef{StarProductRankLayer}\).  Therefore \(x=(a,b)\) determines an
edge counted by \(e_{G(t,K)}(P_r,Z_r)\).

This map from legal poor rank-\(r\) children to ordered edges between
\(P_r\) and \(Z_r\) is injective, because a child of the product digraph is
the ordered pair \((a,b)\).  Hence the number of such children is at most
\[
  e_{G(t,K)}(P_r,Z_r).
\]
By \(\noderef{StarProductPoorRankEdgeBound}\), this edge count is at most
\(5000q^t\).  Thus the legal poor children of rank \(r\) are at most
\(5000q^t\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
