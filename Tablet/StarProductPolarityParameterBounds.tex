\begin{helper}
Let \(K\) be a finite field of order \(q\), and let \(t\ge2\).  Define
\[
  n=\frac{q^{t+1}-1}{q-1},\qquad
  d=\frac{q^t-1}{q-1},\qquad
  a=\frac{q^{t-1}-1}{q-1},
\]
and let \(\lambda=\sqrt{d-a}\), with \(d-a\) interpreted as the natural
number subtraction of these displayed parameters.  Then
\[
  q\ge2,\qquad 0<n,\qquad 0<d,
\]
\[
  q^t\le n\le2q^t,\qquad
  q^{t-1}\le d\le2q^{t-1},
\]
and
\[
  d-a=q^{t-1},\qquad \lambda^2=q^{t-1}.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Since \(K\) is a field, \(0\ne1\), so a finite field has at least two
elements.  Hence \(q=|K|\ge2\).

For \(q\ge2\), the usual finite geometric-sum identity gives
\[
  n=\sum_{i=0}^{t}q^i,\qquad
  d=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}q^i,\qquad
  a=\sum_{i=0}^{t-2}q^i.
\]
Therefore
\[
  n=d+q^t,\qquad d=a+q^{t-1}.
\]
The displayed identities immediately imply \(q^t\le n\) and
\(q^{t-1}\le d\), and they also give
\[
  d-a=q^{t-1}.
\]

It remains to prove the upper bounds.  Since \(q\ge2\), the sum of all
terms below the top term in a geometric progression is strictly smaller
than the top term:
\[
  \sum_{i=0}^{t-1}q^i<q^t,\qquad
  \sum_{i=0}^{t-2}q^i<q^{t-1}.
\]
Using \(n=d+q^t\) and \(d=a+q^{t-1}\), these inequalities yield
\[
  n\le2q^t,\qquad d\le2q^{t-1}.
\]
The lower bounds also show \(n>0\) and \(d>0\).  Finally,
\(\lambda=\sqrt{d-a}\) and \(d-a=q^{t-1}\); since the radicand is
nonnegative, the square-root identity gives
\[
  \lambda^2=q^{t-1}.
\]
\end{proof}
