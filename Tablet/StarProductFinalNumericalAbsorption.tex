\begin{helper}
Let \(A,C\in\mathbb R\) and let \(t,q,k,w\) be nonnegative integers.  Suppose
\[
  Aq^t\ge0,\qquad Aq^t\ge1,
\]
\[
  \bigl(4q^{2t-1}\bigr)^w\le q^{2tw},
  \qquad
  q^{2tw}\le2^k,
  \qquad
  4A\le C.
\]
Then
\[
  2^k\bigl(4q^{2t-1}\bigr)^w(Aq^t)^{k-w}\le (Cq^t)^k.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Put \(B=Aq^t\).  By hypothesis \(B\ge1\), so in particular \(B\ge0\), and
\[
  B^{k-w}\le B^k
\]
because \(k-w\le k\) for natural-number subtraction.  Using the two supplied
power bounds, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
  2^k(4q^{2t-1})^w B^{k-w}
  &\le 2^k q^{2tw} B^{k-w} \\
  &\le 2^k2^k B^{k-w} \\
  &\le 2^k2^k B^k.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last expression is
\[
  2^k2^kB^k=(4B)^k=(4Aq^t)^k.
\]
Since \(4A\le C\) and \(q^t\ge0\),
\[
  4Aq^t\le Cq^t.
\]
Raising to the \(k\)-th power gives
\[
  (4Aq^t)^k\le(Cq^t)^k.
\]
Combining the displayed inequalities proves the claim.
\end{proof}
