\begin{helper}
Let \(C>0\).  There are constants \(c>0\) and \(K_0\) such that for every
integer \(k\) with \(k\ge K_0\), there is an integer \(m\ge1\) with the
following properties.  If \(q=2^m\) and \(K=\mathrm{GaloisField}(2,m)\), then
\[
  |K|=q,\qquad Cq(\log q)^2\le k\le 4Cq(\log q)^2,
\]
\[
  C\le q,\qquad q\ge4,\qquad \log q\le\log k,\qquad
  c\frac{k}{(\log k)^2}\le q .
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
  c=\frac1{2C}>0.
\]
We shall choose \(K_0\) large enough that all the following estimates hold
for every real \(x\ge K_0\).  First require
\[
  1\le x,\qquad 1\le\log x,\qquad \frac1C\le\log x.
\]
Next, using \((\log x)^2=o(x)\), require
\[
  (\log x)^2\le \frac{x}{2C^2},
  \qquad
  (\log x)^2\le \frac{x}{8C}.
\]
Finally, using \(\log\log x=o(\log x)\) and the fact that the constant
\(\log(2C)\) is \(o(\log x)\), require
\[
  \log(2C)+2\log\log x\le \frac14\log x.
\]

Now fix an integer \(k\) with \(k\ge K_0\), and put
\[
  y=\frac{k}{2C(\log k)^2}.
\]
The two estimates for \((\log k)^2\) give \(y\ge C\) and \(y\ge4\).  Choose
\(n\ge0\) with
\[
  2^n\le y<2^{n+1}.
\]
This is the standard dyadic choice of a power of two below \(y\).  Set
\[
  m=n+1,\qquad q=2^m.
\]
Then \(m\ge1\) and
\[
  y\le q\le2y=\frac{k}{C(\log k)^2}. \tag{1}
\]
Since \(q\ge y\), the inequalities \(C\le q\), \(q\ge4\), and
\[
  \frac1{2C}\frac{k}{(\log k)^2}\le q
\]
follow immediately.  The last displayed inequality is exactly the asserted
lower comparison with the chosen value of \(c\).

Because \(m\ne0\), the standard cardinality formula for Galois fields gives
\[
  |\mathrm{GaloisField}(2,m)|=2^m=q.
\]
Also, the upper bound in (1) and \(1\le C(\log k)^2\) give \(q\le k\).  Hence
monotonicity of the logarithm yields \(\log q\le\log k\).

It remains to check the two scale inequalities.  From the upper bound in
(1) and \(\log q\le\log k\),
\[
  Cq(\log q)^2
  \le C\frac{k}{C(\log k)^2}(\log k)^2
  = k.
\]
For the other direction, the lower bound \(y\le q\) gives
\[
  k\le 2Cq(\log k)^2. \tag{2}
\]
The logarithmic large-\(k\) estimate gives
\[
  \log y
  =\log k-\log(2C)-2\log\log k
  \ge \frac34\log k.
\]
Since \(y\le q\), this implies
\[
  \log k\le \frac43\log q.
\]
Therefore
\[
  (\log k)^2\le \frac{16}{9}(\log q)^2\le2(\log q)^2.
\]
Combining this with (2) gives
\[
  k\le4Cq(\log q)^2,
\]
as required.
\end{proof}
