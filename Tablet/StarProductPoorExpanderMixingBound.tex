\begin{helper}
Let \(q>0\), let \(X,Y,e_\ell,e_r,L\) be real numbers with \(X\ge0\) and
\(Y\le X\).  Suppose the poor-vertex definition and the first expander-mixing
lower bound give
\[
  e_\ell\le X/(8q),
  \qquad
  X/(4q)-L\sqrt X\le e_\ell.
\]
Assume also \(L\ge0\) and
\[
  64q^2L^2\le1024q^{t+1}.
\]
Finally suppose the second expander-mixing application gives
\[
  e_r\le (4/q)Y+L\sqrt Y
\]
and its square-root term satisfies
\[
  L\sqrt{1024q^{t+1}}\le904q^t.
\]
Then
\[
  e_r\le5000q^t.
\]
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Combining the first two displayed inequalities gives
\[
  X/(4q)-L\sqrt X\le X/(8q),
\]
and hence
\[
  X/(8q)\le L\sqrt X.
\]
If \(X=0\), then \(X\le1024q^{t+1}\) is immediate.  Otherwise
\(\sqrt X>0\).  Multiplying by \(8q>0\) and dividing by \(\sqrt X\) gives
\[
  \sqrt X\le 8qL.
\]
Squaring and using \(64q^2L^2\le1024q^{t+1}\), we obtain
\[
  X\le (8qL)^2=64q^2L^2\le1024q^{t+1}. \tag{1}
\]
Since \(Y\le X\), (1) also gives \(Y\le1024q^{t+1}\).  Therefore
\[
  (4/q)Y\le (4/q)\,1024q^{t+1}=4096q^t.
\]
Moreover \(L\ge0\) and monotonicity of the square root give
\[
  L\sqrt Y\le L\sqrt{1024q^{t+1}}\le904q^t.
\]
Substituting these two bounds into the second mixing upper bound yields
\[
  e_r\le4096q^t+904q^t=5000q^t,
\]
as claimed.
\end{proof}
